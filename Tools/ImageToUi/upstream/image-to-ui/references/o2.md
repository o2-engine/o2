# o2 Prototype Export

Read this before claiming how the finished `ui_structure.json` behaves in the
game. The editor that runs this skill turns the checked structure into an o2
prototype (`.proto`) in the project's `Assets`, next to its resources:

```
<folder>/<name>.proto
<folder>/<name>_Resources/Sprites/<asset>.png   (+ .meta with slice borders)
<folder>/<name>_Resources/Fonts/<font>.ttf      (+ the font's licence)
<folder>/<name>_Resources/FontStyles/*.fntstyle (text effects)
```

The export is editor-only: native o2 widgets and ordinary assets, no runtime
controller and no JSON interpreter. Re-exporting keeps asset uids and widget
ids.

## Elements

- `container` → `Widget`; with a grid layout o2 can reproduce, `GridLayout`.
- `image` → `Image` widget. A sliced sprite draws in sliced mode with the
  borders the node asks for (`nineSlice`), the image asset's meta keeps the
  sprite's own borders. `color` tints the sprite.
- `rect` / `overlay`, and a container or button with only a `color` → a white
  sprite tinted with that colour.
- `text` → `Label`. Its font comes from the prototype's `Fonts` folder, its
  height is `round(fontSize * 72 / 92)` points, `alignment` / `textVAlign`
  align the glyphs, overflow is off. Stroke and the other text effects become
  a FontStyle asset.
- `button` → `Button` with its sprite as the back layer.
- The `fill` child of a `progress` block → `HorizontalProgress` /
  `VerticalProgress` with the direction and the initial value.
- `opacity` is the element's own; `visible: false` disables the widget.

## Fonts

A `fontFamily` that names a bundled UI font file (`Roboto-Regular.ttf`,
`Roboto-Medium.ttf`, `Roboto-Bold.ttf`, `Lobster-Regular.ttf`,
`FiraSans-Bold.ttf`, ...) is copied into `Fonts` with its licence and the
label uses it. Any other font is drawn with Roboto of the nearest weight
(bold, medium or regular), and the export warns about it.

## Geometry

- `responsive` becomes o2 anchors and offsets (y up); otherwise the box is
  attached to its `anchor` point at the reference size.
- `rotation` turns the widget about the centre of its box: its pivot moves to
  the centre, anchors and offsets keep the box.
- A `grid` with one spacing for both axes and no per-cell `offset` /
  `responsive` becomes a native `GridLayout` (fixed columns, equal cells).
  Rows, columns, other grids and distributed gaps (`space-between/around/
  evenly`, `spacing: "even"`) are baked as positions at the reference size:
  they do not reflow when items are added, removed or resized.

## Baked or left out (the export warns)

- `state`: only `state.current` is exported; the other variants are not.
- `scroll`: the viewport is baked at its offset, without a ScrollArea.
- `hueShift`, `lineHeight`, `textScaleX` and the safe area
  (`responsive.safeArea`) are not applied.
- Children of a progress fill are not clipped.
- A missing sprite leaves its widget without a picture.

Application code owns events, data bindings, visibility changes and progress
updates. Font rasterization and effects in o2 may differ from the Python
previews; do not promise pixel-for-pixel agreement.
